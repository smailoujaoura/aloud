The lighthouse keeper's log

Every evening at dusk, the keeper climbed the spiral stairs to light the lamp. He wrote the weather in a ledger that nobody else would ever read. Wind from the west, visibility good, two boats heading north. The entries are short, but together they make a kind of diary of the sea.

Bell Rock Lighthouse[edit]

Location: North Sea, off Angus
Built: 1807 to 1810
Height: 35 m

The Bell Rock Lighthouse is the oldest surviving sea-washed lighthouse in the world.[1] It was built between 1807 and 1810 on a reef in the North Sea, eleven miles off the coast of Scotland.[2][3]

Follow along

Watch the words light up as you listen.

The sentence being read is shaded right where it sits, and the word inside it is marked as it's spoken. The page glides along with the voice. Scroll off to look at something and Aloud waits for you, then catches up.

Word by word

A deeper mark on the word being spoken. Pick its colour and how strong it is.

Following, your way

Keep the sentence in view, snap it to the middle, or never scroll at all. F turns following on or off.

Your highlights stay yours

Passages you kept keep their colours while Aloud reads over them.

Easy on dark pages

The highlight adapts to the page, so the text under it stays easy to read.

Chrome and Firefox do equally well: what matters is whether the voice can reach your graphics card through WebGPU. Chrome has it on already. Firefox keeps it off for now, and it takes a minute to turn on. Aloud's settings test your machine and tell you which case you're in. Why it's safe, and how to turn it on in every browser →

Turn on WebGPU in Firefox
  1. Open a new tab, type about:config into the address bar, and accept the warning.
  2. Search for dom.webgpu.enabled and set it to true. This turns WebGPU on.
  3. Search for dom.webgpu.workers.enabled and set it to true. This is the one that matters: Aloud makes its voice in a background worker, and this lets the worker use the graphics card.
  4. Only if hardware acceleration is off on your machine: set gfx.webrender.all to true.
  5. Reload the page you're reading. Aloud's settings should now say the voice runs on your graphics card; if they don't, restart Firefox.

To undo it, search for the same names and click the reset arrow next to each.

Keep what matters

Keep the passages you'll want again.

Select some text and press K, or pick a colour from the little toolbar. The passage stays highlighted on the page, and it's highlighted again whenever you come back. While Aloud reads, N keeps the sentence you just heard.

Highlight or underline

Five colours, in either style. Your marks look the way you left them.

Notes and tags

Write a line about it and add a tag or two: type one, press Enter, and it's there.

Find it again

Search everything, or filter by tag, colour, site, today or this week, or just the notes you wrote something on.

Take them with you

Copy them as Markdown, links included, or export everything in one file.

Come back later

Close the tab. Aloud remembers.

Stop in the middle of a sentence and come back tomorrow: Aloud starts at that sentence, checked against the text, so an edited page never sends you to the wrong place.

Continue listening

Everything you stopped part-way through, with how much is left. One click picks it up where you were.

Up next

Queue articles for later. They play one after another in the Library, even offline, or on their own page if you'd rather.

PDFs, in the Library

Open a PDF from your computer or the web: real pages with figures and columns, sharp zoom, a page counter, and marks saved into the file.

Take it with you

Save a whole article or PDF as one MP3, in your voice and speed, for a walk or a commute. And a sleep timer for reading in bed.

Your listening, counted

Words heard, time listened and documents finished, by day, site and language. Only what you actually heard counts.

Videos too

The same speed keys for any video on the web: D faster, S slower, Z and X to rewind and skip, even on Shorts. Remembered per site. Start it with Alt+M.

8

Yours alone

The voice lives on your device. So does everything you read.

Not a promise, a design: there is no Aloud server to send anything to. Here's the complete list of what ever touches the network.

Never leaves this device

  • The speech itself: every sentence is made right here
  • Your highlights and notes, and the tags on them
  • Where you stopped in every article and PDF
  • Your reading queue, history and listening statistics
  • Settings, keys, colours and per-site video speeds

Goes out only when you ask

  • Downloading a voice, once, from Hugging Face
  • Previewing a Piper voice you haven't downloaded: a short hosted clip
  • Fetching a page or PDF you told Aloud to read or queue

A saved article's images stay unfetched until you press Load images, so reopening one tells nobody you're reading it. Aloud only runs on a site when you start it there, or on sites you list yourself.
